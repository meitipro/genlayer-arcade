---
title: Limitations
description: What Unban decides, and what it honestly cannot.
---

<Callout title="Honest limits">
Unban decides whether the record shows a written rule broken and whether the penalty fits the ladder, not what really happened
in the chat. It trusts the quoted evidence unless the record contradicts itself, it covers a community's own rules rather than
platform terms, and it only works as well as the rules a community is willing to write down.
</Callout>

| Attack or risk | What happens | The answer in the design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spam appeals | Banned raiders appeal in bulk | Each costs the fee, which an upheld ruling sends to the pool, and one appeal per address per court per week |
| Moderators ignoring appeals | Nobody answers | DEFAULTED after the window, the ban is lifted and the pool pays; response times are public |
| Fabricated evidence | A moderator invents a quote | The judge cannot check chat logs; the quote sits in public next to the moderator's address and the member's reply disputes it |
| Rules written after the fact | A rule is added to justify an old ban | Rules freeze while appeals are open and apply only to bans dated after a change |
| A court that pays nothing | The owner never funds the pool | The court page shows the pool, and appeals still refund the fee when the member is right |
| Ignoring the ruling | The community keeps the ban anyway | The bot applies rulings automatically; without it, the ruling and the refusal are both public |
| Prompt injection | An appeal or a quote tells the judge what to rule | Fenced as data, ignored by rule, judged on the conduct in the record; golden case 9 tests it |
| A new address | A member appeals from a fresh wallet to look like a first offender | Earlier rulings are counted per address; the moderator's quotes and the member's handle are still public |

Not built yet: evidence locked by hash at ban time, Telegram and X adapters for the bot, shared rulebooks, and rulings shown on
a member's GenLayer Identity card.
